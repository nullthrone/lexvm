# 0010. Content licensing

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-10-23

> This ADR records a project policy. It is not legal advice. Contributors who
> are unsure about their rights should consult a qualified lawyer.

## Context

LexVM separates code from content:

- The code is licensed under the [MIT License](../../LICENSE).
- Every content pack declares its own license as an SPDX identifier in
  `license` ([specification](../spec/content-pack.md#3-top-level)). The demo
  pack `locked-study` is `CC0-1.0`.

Content packs are distributed in several ways: in the repository, bundled
into the player on GitHub Pages, inside `lexvm-offline.html`, as release
assets, and as files that players pass on. A license must allow all of this
without asking each author.

Many packs will be written with LLMs ([ADR 0007](0007-llm-pipeline-byo-prompt-kit-and-api-generator.md)).
This raises open questions:

- In several jurisdictions copyright requires human authorship. The US
  Copyright Office, for example, does not register material generated by AI
  without sufficient human creative control, and German law protects only a
  "personal intellectual creation" (section 2(2) of the German Copyright Act,
  UrhG). Purely generated text may therefore not be protected at all, while
  human selection, arrangement and editing may be.
- A license declared on a pack can only license the rights that exist. For
  largely generated packs, the declaration may be partly without effect.
- LLM providers' terms of use may say something about the ownership and use
  of outputs.
- LLM output can, rarely, reproduce existing copyrighted text.

The demo pack is also embedded in the prompt kit as an example, so LLM output
may resemble it.

## Decision (proposed)

1. **Each pack declares its own license.** `license` is required and must be
   a valid SPDX identifier.
2. **Recommended default for community packs: `CC-BY-4.0`.** It allows
   redistribution and adaptation (including bundling into the player and
   releases) and keeps credit for authors. Authors may choose another
   license that permits redistribution by the project, such as `CC0-1.0` or
   `CC-BY-SA-4.0`.
3. **The demo pack stays `CC0-1.0`**, so that packs inspired by the reference
   example in the prompt kit carry no attribution obligations.
4. **Packs in this repository must be redistributable** by the project in
   all the ways listed above. Licenses with non-commercial or no-derivatives
   terms are not accepted into the repository.
5. **Provenance is disclosed.** `author` and `generator` state honestly how
   the pack was written, including LLM involvement and human editing.
6. **No third-party material.** Packs must not copy text from other works or
   use characters or settings from copyrighted franchises. Contributors
   confirm that they have the right to license the content.
7. **Inbound equals outbound.** A contributed pack is accepted under the
   license it declares; there is no contributor license agreement.

## Consequences

- Bundling and redistribution are covered by default; authors keep credit.
- Players and other projects can reuse community packs with attribution.
- The license field may overstate protection for largely generated content;
  disclosure of provenance makes this visible instead of hiding it.
- Attribution under CC BY must be shown somewhere; the player should display
  `author` and `license` for each pack (library or pack details).
- Reviewers must check license and provenance for every content pack.

## Open questions

- How far is LLM-assisted content protected by copyright in the
  jurisdictions that matter for contributors, and does that change which
  default makes sense (for example `CC0-1.0` for largely generated packs)?
- Should the project require a minimum level of human editing for
  LLM-generated packs?
- Do we need a structured provenance field (for example
  `generator.humanEdited`) instead of free text, and should it be signed in
  the community pipeline (v0.3)?
- How should packs derived from other packs record their sources?

## Alternatives considered

- **`CC0-1.0` as the default.** Maximum freedom and no doubt about rights
  that may not exist for generated text, but authors lose credit.
- **`CC-BY-SA-4.0` as the default.** Keeps derivatives open, but share-alike
  creates compatibility friction for bundling and remixing.
- **A software license (MIT) for content.** Possible, but designed for code,
  not prose; Creative Commons licenses are the established choice for
  creative works.
- **One license for all packs, set by the project.** Simpler to review, but
  takes the choice away from authors and does not fit packs from other
  sources.
- **No recommendation.** Leaves authors without guidance and leads to
  licenses that do not allow bundling.
