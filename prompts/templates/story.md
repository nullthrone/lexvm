# Stage 2 of 5: story

Write the **story** of the adventure described in the concept below: the narrative backbone that the later stages turn into puzzles, rooms, dialogue and rule messages. Pack language: {{LANG}}; target play time: about {{MINUTES}} minutes; pacing: {{PACING}}.

{{BUDGET}}

Why this stage exists: the player only ever reads the game text. What happened, who wants what and why, and how the player character comes to understand it must end up in that text, not stay in the design notes. Write the story as scenes now, so the later stages can cut it into the data model instead of inventing text one field at a time.

Write a Markdown document with these sections:

1. **The truth:** what really happened before and during the game, in causal order, with every motive (who did what, and why). Short paragraphs, in English or the pack language.
2. **Through-line:** what the player character wants, what they believe at the start, what they understand at the end, and the open question that pulls the player from scene to scene.
3. **Scene plan:** the scenes in the order a typical play meets them (chapters may hold scenes the player can take in any order — say so). Per scene: place, who is there, the *arrival* (what the player sees and what is at stake), the *turn* (what is learned or changes, in a fact or a relationship), and the *way out* (why the player goes on, and where). Mark the turning point of every major conversation.
4. **Revelation map:** a table — every fact of section 1 that the ending relies on, the scene where the player learns it, and the source (a dialogue line, a document, a deduction, the player character's own conclusion). A fact without a scene does not exist for the player.
5. **Key scenes in prose:** in the pack language, written as the player will read them, without length limits: the intro, the first arrival at the central location, the turning point of every major conversation, the climax and the ending. Later stages split them into room texts, dialogue nodes and rule messages; keep them close to that shape (narration in second person, present tense, as the guide says for the language).
6. **Voice:** the narrator's stance, the player character's inner voice (what they notice, how they draw conclusions — in moderation), how each character speaks, and three sample paragraphs that show the rhythm: varied sentence lengths, thoughts joined by their logic, no telegram style and no adjective padding.

Answer with the Markdown document only, starting with `# Story`.
